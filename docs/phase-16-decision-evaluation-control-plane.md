# Phase 16 — Decision-grade evaluation and calibration control plane

## Scope and decision

Phase 16 implements the measurement foundation required before value-of-information acquisition, corrective learning, graph portfolio adaptation, or provider calibration can be promoted. It does not change a production classification or automatically activate a candidate policy.

The control plane adds:

- deterministic retrieval-cohort assignment with explicit inclusion propensity;
- a protected random-audit lane distinct from targeted evaluation;
- immutable human, delayed-production, and adjudicated ground-truth labels;
- sealed, checksummed, time-split evaluation datasets pinned to production diagnostic snapshots;
- propensity-weighted benchmark metrics, calibration error, Brier score, effective sample size, and confidence intervals;
- immutable calibration artifacts and promotion-gate decisions;
- an inspection endpoint that explicitly reports `servingAuthority: false` and `automaticPromotion: false`.

## Measurement semantics

Review outcomes generated by the serving policy are not assumed to represent the population. Each evaluation example carries its inclusion probability. Aggregate rates use inverse-propensity weights, while effective sample size exposes when concentrated weights make a nominally large dataset unreliable.

The initial benchmark reports:

- positive precision and recall;
- false-positive and false-negative rates;
- abstention/review rate;
- Brier score;
- expected calibration error over ten deterministic bins;
- Wilson precision and recall intervals;
- the same metrics for immutable segment definitions.

Candidate probabilities are fitted with weighted binned isotonic calibration on
the sealed `CALIBRATION` split and are evaluated only on the later `TEST` split.
Insufficient effective sample size in either split produces
`INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`; it is never silently repaired by fitting on test data.

Scores must be probabilities in `[0,1]`. Existing production confidence remains a policy score and must not be supplied as a probability until a versioned mapping is fitted. This prevents the new evaluator from laundering the existing heuristic score into an unsupported calibration claim.

## Promotion contract

A gate compares baseline and candidate benchmark artifacts using predeclared constraints:

- minimum effective sample size;
- minimum terminal-positive precision lower bound;
- maximum recall regression;
- maximum abstention increase;
- maximum Brier-score regression;
- sufficient evidence in every required language/script/evidence segment.

`PROMOTE` is evidence that a predeclared gate passed; it is not serving authority. Existing approval, canary, feature flag, immutable publication, and rollback mechanisms remain authoritative.

## Ground-truth integration

Committed approve/reject review decisions append evaluation labels asynchronously after the authoritative review transaction, matching the failure isolation of adaptive-shadow labeling. A failed observer cannot roll back or alter the human decision. Labels reference the immutable review decision and preserve its evidence snapshot.

Future adjudication can append a new label without updating the older one. Dataset construction selects and pins the label under an explicit dataset definition, so historical benchmark results remain reproducible.

## Rollout and operational cost

- The schema is additive and all new ledgers are immutable.
- No production decision reads evaluation tables.
- No provider call is introduced by the initial phase.
- Serving latency impact is limited to a fire-and-forget label observer after human decisions.
- Dataset materialization and benchmark execution are offline work.
- Review quota must reserve a protected random sample before targeted active learning is enabled.

## Next dependency

The value-of-information controller may use this control plane only after retrieval-boundary assignment and sealed dataset materialization are wired for its canary population. Its learned action-value estimates must retain assignment propensity and compare against the fixed enrichment policy.
